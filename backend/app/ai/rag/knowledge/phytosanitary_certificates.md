# Phytosanitary Certificates & Plant Quarantine Import Controls Guide

## 1. Regulatory Context: International Plant Protection Convention (IPPC)
The International Plant Protection Convention (IPPC) is an international treaty overseen by the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) aimed at preventing the introduction and spread of plant pests and diseases across international borders.

## 2. Phytosanitary Certificate (PSC) Purpose and Issuance
A Phytosanitary Certificate is an official document issued by the National Plant Protection Organization (NPPO) of the exporting country (e.g., Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine & Storage in India; USDA APHIS in the United States).
- Certifies that the inspected consignment of plants, plant products, spices, or seeds:
  - Has been inspected and tested according to appropriate procedures.
  - Is considered free from quarantine pests specified by the importing country.
  - Conforms to the current phytosanitary regulations of the importing country.

## 3. Fumigation and Treatment Endorsements
Many importing jurisdictions mandate pre-shipment treatment for agricultural consignments:
- **Methyl Bromide Fumigation / Phosphine (Aluminum Phosphide):** Required to eradicate grain borers, beetles, and mites in dry bulk spices and grains.
- **ISPM 15 Wood Packaging Standard:** Any solid wood packaging material (pallets, dunnage, crating) must be heat-treated (HT) or fumigated and stamped with the official IPPC wheat-sheaf logo. Non-compliant pallets are subject to immediate border quarantine refusal or re-export at shipper expense.
- The Phytosanitary Certificate must record the treatment date, chemical dosage, duration, and ambient temperature in Box 11.

## 4. Key Rejection Triggers at European and North American Ports
1. Discrepancies between botanical Latin binomial names (e.g., *Curcuma longa* for turmeric) and trade descriptions.
2. Expired validity: certificates typically remain valid for shipping only within 14 days of inspection and issuance.
3. Handwritten alterations without official NPPO validation stamps.
4. Presence of prohibited weed seeds, foreign matter, soil particles, or live insect larvae during border sampling.
