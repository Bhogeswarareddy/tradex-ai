# US FDA Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA) & Import Compliance

## 1. Overview of FSMA
The Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA), enacted into law in the United States, shifted the food safety focus from responding to contamination to preventing it. It applies to both domestic and international facilities that process, pack, or hold food for human or animal consumption in the US.

## 2. Foreign Supplier Verification Programs (FSVP)
- Under 21 CFR 1, Subpart L, US importers are legally responsible for verifying that their foreign food suppliers meet FDA safety standards:
  - Food is produced in compliance with Preventive Controls for Human Food (21 CFR Part 117).
  - Food is not adulterated under section 402 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act).
  - Food is not misbranded with respect to food allergen labeling.
- Importers must maintain a formal FSVP plan for each foreign supplier and food product, containing hazard analyses, evaluation of supplier food safety performance, and annual onsite audits or testing verification.

## 3. FDA Facility Registration & US Agent Requirement
- Any foreign manufacturer or processor supplying food or dietary ingredients to the US must register with the FDA and renew every even-numbered year between October 1 and December 31.
- Foreign facilities must appoint a designated **US Agent** located physically in the United States who serves as the liaison for emergency communications and inspection scheduling.
- A unique 9-digit Data Universal Numbering System (**DUNS**) or Unique Facility Identifier (UFI) recognized by the FDA is required during registration.

## 4. Prior Notice of Imported Food
- Food shipments entering the US require electronic **Prior Notice (PN)** submission to the FDA via the Automated Commercial Environment (ACE) or FDA Prior Notice System Interface (PNSI).
- Submission timelines:
  - Ocean vessel: No less than 8 hours before port arrival.
  - Air transport: No less than 4 hours before arrival.
  - Road / Truck: No less than 2 hours before border crossing.
- Shipments arriving without confirmed Prior Notice are held at the port of entry under Section 801(m) of the FD&C Act and cannot be moved without authorization.
