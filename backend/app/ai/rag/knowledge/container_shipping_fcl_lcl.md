# Containerized Ocean Shipping: FCL vs LCL, Equipment Specifications & Demurrage

## 1. FCL (Full Container Load) vs LCL (Less than Container Load)
- **FCL (Full Container Load):** An entire ocean container is booked by and dedicated to a single shipper and consignee.
  - Advantages: Faster transit time (no deconsolidation delay at CFS), minimal handling damage risk, sealed container door integrity from origin factory to destination warehouse.
  - Recommended for order quantities $\ge 10 \text{ MT}$ of spices, grains, or bulk botanicals.
- **LCL (Less than Container Load):** Cargo from multiple shippers sharing the same destination port is consolidated into a shared container at a Container Freight Station (CFS).
  - Advantages: Cost-effective for sample consignments, test orders, or high-value botanical extracts under 5,000 kg.
  - Risks: Elevated risk of cross-contamination from adjacent cargo (e.g. chemical odors tainting food-grade organic spices), additional CFS handling fees.

## 3. Container Desiccant & Moisture Prevention
Agricultural cargo emits moisture during ocean transit through warm tropical waters to cold European ports ("container rain" or condensation).
- **Best Practice:** Install calcium chloride moisture-absorbing desiccant poles (minimum 1 kg per ton of cargo) and utilize breathable container ceiling liners to prevent mold damage.

## 4. Demurrage and Detention Rules
- **Demurrage:** Fee charged by the shipping line when an import container remains inside the port terminal beyond the agreed contractual free time (typically 5 to 7 free days).
- **Detention:** Fee charged when the container has been picked up from the port terminal by the consignee's trucker but is not returned empty to the carrier's inland container yard within the allowed free days (typically 5 to 10 days).
- Daily rates escalate rapidly from €75 to €250+ per container per day. Always negotiate extended free time (14-21 combined days) when finalizing ocean freight contracts for commodities requiring customs lab tests.
